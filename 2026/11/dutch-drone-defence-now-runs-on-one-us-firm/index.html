Dutch drone defence now runs on one US firm's software, bought without a race

Parliament stayed out, rivals stayed out, and a Dutch or European software answer exists so far only as the ministry's stated option. Experts warned of lost autonomy.

6 November 2026

The Dutch state bought its new drone defence, and the seller broke the news. On 5 July, Anduril announced on its website that the Dutch Ministry of Defence had awarded it the contract, according to Follow the Money. The company told the world in its own words, on the day the deal went public. The buyers in The Hague said nothing.

In March, the ministry signed a contract with Anduril for software and sensor technology to detect, track, identify and neutralize unmanned aircraft. The House of Representatives was not told. The contract's value sat under 50 million euros, below the threshold that requires parliament be informed.

Follow the Money reported in November 2026 that the ministry had secretly concluded several contracts with Anduril in recent months.

In March 2026, the official record quoted US Secretary of War Pete Hegseth saying that the AI of the Ministry of War would not be woke.

Speed and silence

The speed was a choice, and the records show how it was made. The ministry picked procedures in which speed and silence were the same decision.

Anduril's Lattice platform now connects the whole anti-drone chain, according to the Ministry of Defence. By the company's own account, its products went from contract signing to initial operational use within about a month. Anduril opened its Dutch presence in April.

On 13 July 2026, the ministry awarded Surcom International B.V. of Ede a contract for command-and-control systems, including Anduril Voyager products. The contract was worth 3,050,000.00 euros, excluding VAT.

That order went through a negotiated procedure without any prior call for competition. The reason given: an urgent need for delivery by a fixed date, arising from a crisis. Exactly one tender came in. No review request was filed.

A ministry spokesperson said multiple providers were considered for the anti-drone software. Anduril's solution was available the fastest, and there was an 'operational necessity' to use and evaluate a defence system as quickly as possible.

Dependence on American technology

Experts cited by Follow the Money called the cooperation unwise and unnecessary. It deepens dependence on American technology, they said, though European alternatives exist. They warned that Anduril staff could gain potential access to sensitive military data. dgki.nl wrote that vital national security tasks are becoming increasingly dependent on foreign commercial algorithms.

In March 2026, the ministry told the House it planned to sign the American CCA programme's acceptance letter. The parliamentary questions of 23 March 2026 noted it. The budget recorded there was 50 to 100 million euros.

The Americans asked for a contribution equal to the purchase of two test aircraft. The aircraft stay US property, while the Netherlands buys access to the test data and the analysis.

The Dutch defence minister said in a letter to parliament, as an exception, because he attaches extra value to transparency in this case, that he informed the House in advance. The purchase of the two aircraft was missing from an earlier letter to parliament.

Defensie said the open architecture of the American programme leaves it free to build software later with Dutch and European partners, to Dutch or European standards. Defensie said the knowledge from CCA could also serve possible future participation in programmes with European partners.

The ministry set aside about 950 million euros for military innovation, much less than the 2.89 billion long communicated.

A US Air Force spokesperson said in April the two aircraft could be the General Atomics YFQ-42A, Anduril's YFQ-44A, or one of each. The YFQ-42 and YFQ-44 make up increment one of the CCA programme.

The US report of April describes what the collaboration buys: the two air forces develop autonomous capabilities together on one open architecture system, for seamless data sharing and interoperability with partners in combined operations.

Anduril's chief executive, Palmer Luckey, said there is no moral high ground in using inferior technology, even if it allows you to say things like: "We never let a robot decide who lives and who dies."

One American company's software

First, the chain that keeps drones from Dutch bases now runs on one American company's software. A contract below the reporting threshold kept parliament out. A negotiated order without competition kept other sellers out.

Experts warned that with the deals the Netherlands is handing away its own digital and strategic autonomy. Second, the answer the ministry offers, building software later with Dutch and European partners to Dutch or European standards, exists so far only as Defensie's stated option. On paper, the Netherlands has signed the declaration on responsible military AI; the United States has not.

The home-grown answer opened as a small, temporary forerunner under the Dutch research organisation TNO, far below the money once promised for military innovation.

The open question stands in writing since 23 March 2026. MP Jagtenberg of D66 asked what the plan is if the CCA programme's AI does not align with European or Dutch standards.

The State Secretary had told parliament days earlier, on 19 March, that the Netherlands intended to sign the Letter of Acceptance on 8 April. The signature was planned. The answer to Jagtenberg's question has not come.
