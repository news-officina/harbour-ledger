Hackers say stolen Rosstat data shows 450,100 Russian military deaths

The claim rests on leaked death certificates that Rosstat says were not compromised. At least 168 public datasets have vanished from the agency's collections.

9 November 2026

Silent Crow says death records it stole from Rosstat show 450,100 Russian military deaths since February 2022. Two hacker groups, Silent Crow and the Belarusian Cyber Partisans, say they took about 23 terabytes of data from Rosstat and wrecked part of its IT systems. Rosstat says it found no evidence its databases were compromised.

Rosstat admitted cyber attacks hit its systems between October 29 and November 1. The agency said no data leaked, but reported disruption to some services and gave statisticians extra time to file their numbers. At least 168 datasets have vanished from its public collections, covering civil servants' pay, social benefits, state assistance, combat veterans and funeral payments. Janis Kluge of the German Institute for International and Security Affairs called it the most significant data leak on Russian casualties to date.

The tally rests on the leaked death certificates. Silent Crow said 356,056 carry code Y36, meaning death by operations of war, and 38,229 carry combat wording or code Y89.1. It counted another 14,160 non-combat deaths of servicemen. It added 9,118 records with no stated cause and smaller groups flagged by other fields, leaving out the wounded, the missing and foreign fighters. Deaths in the dataset rose from 18,877 in 2022 to 42,365 in 2023, 109,683 in 2024 and 182,264 in 2025. Another 39,296 are logged so far for the partially recorded 2026.

Correcting for registration lag, Silent Crow puts the real death toll at about 504,280 by August 28. It estimates between 562,979 and 596,888 by the end of 2026. Russia has not published its own losses since September 2022 and calls independent estimates unreliable. Kluge said the deaths equal around half a percent of Russia's working-age population of 80 million. "It's about who is winning this war," he said. "And you cannot say Russia is winning this war if it is losing population at this rate."

The Cyber Partisans say the attack destroyed Rosstat's databases, servers and VPNs. They say recovery will demand significant time and resources. The two groups claimed a hack of Aeroflot in late July 2025. They said they had spent about a year inside the airline's network and destroyed thousands of servers; Russia's Prosecutor General's Office opened a criminal case. On July 21, 2026, Russia's Supreme Court declared both groups "extremist" organizations.
