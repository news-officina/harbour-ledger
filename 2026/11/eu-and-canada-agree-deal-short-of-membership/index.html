EU and Canada agree deal short of membership

Diplomats say the deal drops the 'associate member' label Canada wanted, after Germany objected. It promises fewer trade barriers and a study on more Canadian LNG exports to Europe.

2 November 2026

The EU and Canada have agreed at their Montreal summit on an 'Alliance for the Future' and an 'ever closer association'. It is short of full membership. But the final text, adopted on 28 October, drops the 'associate member' label Canada wanted. Germany broke ranks the day before and called the term a red line, EU diplomats told Politico and CBC. The deal promises fewer trade barriers and a study on extra Canadian LNG exports to Europe.

Council President António Costa called the partnership something new that goes beyond a simple association. He said it covers everything short of full EU membership and is the closest, deepest and most unique of its kind. Costa told reporters at the Montreal summit press conference: "Our relationship is so special that we want to create something new, to go beyond a simple association." Canada will also join support for Ukraine, a decision its Prime Minister Mark Carney announced. The summit declaration welcomed this step; the existing Ukraine support package stands at 90 billion euros.

Still, Carney told the Montreal summit: 'Today, we move towards associate membership for Canada, and we launch a new alliance for the future.' He said the two sides were working towards the deepest possible partnership short of accession, with Canada becoming the EU's first associate member.

The deal is not done. Carney said Canada and the EU aim to conclude talks on associate membership by June 2027. He said the full substance, scale and scope of the new membership would be worked out in the coming months. On the naming dispute, he said the specific name does not matter. What matters, he said, is the difference it makes to Canadians and to Canada becoming more independent and sovereign. Washington pushed back: Trump called the idea ridiculous and threatened high tariffs or ending trade with Europe 'on many things' if it harmed US interests. He said he might interpret it as a 'hostile act'.

Diplomats and Commission officials said Germany blocked the 'associate member' label because it had tried and failed to grant Ukraine the same status. Chancellor Merz had first floated the idea for Kyiv. The final text also lost wording on financial market integration: the explicit goal was dropped. The sides now only promise to limit harm to each other. Far-reaching commitments on mutual preferential treatment in economic security measures were watered down in the same final text.
