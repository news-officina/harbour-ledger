Russian strike on Komyshuvakha aid site, 43 dead and 53 missing

Zelensky says 43 bodies were recovered and 53 people are missing. Rescuers and police died in a second hit on a nearby shelter. The UN has condemned the strikes.

6 November 2026

President Zelensky said on 5 November that 43 bodies had been recovered after the Russian strike on Komyshuvakha and 53 people were still missing. He called the strike proof that Russia "wants no de-escalation."

The bombs hit the site in two waves. First the former school, as people gathered for aid. Then a nearby shelter, where rescuers and police who had come to help took cover. Two rescuers and four police officers died, and several more were hurt. Zelensky said Russia also struck Ukrainian energy infrastructure in a barrage of drones and missiles just after the strike. He said Ukraine needs help defending its skies, or Russia will never stop its missiles.

Mykhailo Semikin, head of the Zaporizhzhia regional military administration, called it the deadliest Russian strike on the Zaporizhzhia region since the start of the full-scale invasion. "The scale of the tragedy is horrific. Behind every number lies a human life," he said. Zelensky said Russia could not have been unaware that residents gathered there for aid every Monday. Rescue work went on for days, with teams and dogs brought in from several regions. Drones struck the area again and hurt at least 17 more people. The state rescue service said attacks and dust repeatedly halted the search.

Matthias Schmale, the UN humanitarian coordinator for Ukraine, condemned the Komyshuvakha strikes. He said civilians seeking support and first responders must never be a target. He said civilian facilities including humanitarian assistance must be respected and protected under international humanitarian law. Danielle Bell, head of the UN human rights monitoring mission in Ukraine, said the attack "seems to have violated the most fundamental principles of international humanitarian law." Russia's defense ministry claims its forces hit Ukrainian military units in and around Komyshuvakha, killing up to 25 servicemen.

Latvia wants the UN Security Council to meet over the strike. Foreign Minister Baiba Braže announced the extraordinary meeting on 3 November, as reported by Kyiv Post. Latvia chairs the council for November, the first time in its history it holds the post, and the meeting comes as US envoy Steve Witkoff said Washington will bring together Ukrainian and Russian delegations in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November to discuss ending the conflict.
