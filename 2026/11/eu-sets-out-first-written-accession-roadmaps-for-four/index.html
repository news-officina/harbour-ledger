EU sets out first written accession roadmaps for four candidate countries

The Commission set out paths for Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine and says candidates get access to selected areas of the single market before they join. Serbia made no substantive progress.

4 November 2026

Four countries now have a written path into the European Union. For the first time, the Commission has set out indicative accession roadmaps for Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine. Ursula von der Leyen, the Commission President, announced them in her State of the Union address: "Membership is moving closer for countries that deliver."

Membership is not the only prize. The Commission says candidates get real gains before they join: economic opportunities, better connections to EU networks, and access to selected areas of the single market. The Commission set out these gains in its annual Enlargement Package, published on 4 November. The package's Moldova report assesses how the country's reforms have gone over the last 12 months.

The Commission has put a financial package on the table for Montenegro's accession, and work on the accession treaty has started. The Council adopted conclusions on that package, which the Commission says confirm Montenegro as the frontrunner. The Commission calls the country's progress in the negotiations remarkable. It assesses that Montenegro met the closing benchmarks in all chapters and made decisive progress on the fundamentals, with good results in rule of law, public administration reform, statistics and economic governance.

Moldova sits among the four most advanced candidates in the European Commission's 2026 enlargement report. Over the past year, the country has opened four clusters in its EU accession negotiations, the groups of policy chapters that are negotiated together. The Commission's assessment is that Moldova has met the conditions to open all the remaining clusters of chapters. It also judges Moldova's target of closing the negotiations by early 2028 ambitious but achievable.

The Commission found no substantive progress in Serbia's accession talks, with negative trends on the fundamentals and low alignment with EU foreign policy. Elections on 25 October brought the Student List, born of the protest movement, to power with about 53% of the vote. Kosovo risks losing more than 40 million euros in growth plan funds after missing the June deadline, Marta Kos, the EU enlargement commissioner, said, though reforms have intensified.
