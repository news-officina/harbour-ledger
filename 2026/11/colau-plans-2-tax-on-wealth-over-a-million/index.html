Colau plans 2% tax on wealth over a million euros

Her Frente Amplio says the Barcelona plan would push more than 500,000 homes back onto the market. Rents hit by it would fall 31% on average.

8 November 2026

Ada Colau, the Frente Amplio candidate, has launched the party's Barcelona campaign with a plan that hits large fortunes and empty flats. Her party wants an annual tax of 2% on wealth above one million euros. It says more than 500,000 homes would return to the market or cut rents. Affected rents would drop by an average of 31%.

The plan's housing tax goes after the value of the homes themselves. It takes 10% of each property's value. Companies pay it from their first home, individuals from their fourth, when their combined value tops 800,000 euros. Stable affordable rentals are exempt. Sectors linked to Podemos, a Spanish left-wing party, say housing and tax plans like these already appeared in their own electoral programmes. The party points to tensions among forces competing to the left of the PSOE.

Colau launched her attack at the Sagrera rally. People rise each day to go to work, she said, while 13 vulture funds hold more than 100,000 homes in Spain. Colau called that accumulation immoral. The last jab went at Cayetana Álvarez de Toledo, a deputy for the PP, Spain's main conservative party, and the number two on the Madrid list. Colau called her a marchioness and said she and her posh friends would be fried with taxes.

There is no right to wealth, Colau told the rally. She said no human rights charter grants a right to accumulate wealth, only rights to health, to education and to a decent life. She put the case for the tax change in terms of who pays. She proposed revising the tax deductions enjoyed by banks and large business groups. Those groups pay an effective tax rate of 7% to 9%, she said, against 19% or 20% for small and medium firms.

Colau said the 29-N elections should become a referendum on whether housing is a right or a commodity. She accused the PP and Vox, a Spanish far-right party, of defending tax cuts and housing deregulation. On 5 November, Frente Amplio posted on Bluesky that growth should reach people's lives, with those who accumulate the most paying more. The post linked this to its proposal to raise taxes on large fortunes and companies. It did not name her or Vox.
