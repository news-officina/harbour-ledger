Bomb hoaxes disrupt at least 18 flights across nine countries

Aer Lingus, Ryanair and Finnair flights were hit on 6 November. Searches found no explosives and all aircraft landed safely.

6 November 2026

Bomb alerts disrupted flights across Europe on Friday 6 November. At least 18 flights to or from nine countries were hit, and media outlets were targeted too. Ireland's government, led by Taoiseach Micheál Martin, said a coordinated campaign to cause disruption was probable. Searches found no explosives, and all affected aircraft landed safely.

Hungary scrambled Gripen jets and Bulgaria sent up MiGs to escort targeted planes. Bomb disposal teams and dog units worked each aircraft, the BBC and La7 reported. In Italy, Raniero Altavilla's National Anti-Mafia and Anti-Terrorism Prosecutor's Office took charge of the Rome, Milan, Busto Arsizio and Civitavecchia investigations.

Aer Lingus and Ryanair flights, supermarkets and Irish media offices, including RTÉ and Newstalk, had taken bomb threats in the days before. On 5 November, an Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome was diverted to Évreux military air base in France after a threat. The Tuesday and Thursday threats disrupted at least 35 flights in total, Newsweek and The Guardian reported.

An email to newsrooms claimed explosives were planted on a Finnair flight from Helsinki and an Aer Lingus flight from Dublin. It carried the line: "We, fighters of the Male State, will leave a mark on history with our actions today". The same email promised more attacks, Gandul and Ireland Live reported. It said the group would soon blow up planes, cruise ships, metro stations and trains.

Investigators said they found no evidence the hoax threats against airlines and other targets were orchestrated by a hostile terror group or state actor, but their inquiries continue. Vladislav Pozdniakov, the founder of the Male State, denied on Telegram that his group was involved. A Telegram channel linked to the Male State told the BBC Russian Service it had sent no emails. Spain's foreign minister, José Manuel Albares, said Europe was facing an attempt at hybrid aggression, an attack that mixes threats and disruption, and a form of psychological warfare.
